PLAN. APPROVE. EQUIP.

The right equipment for the work ahead.

New equipment should solve a clear business problem. This procedure explains how African Foods employees request equipment and how each purchase is checked, approved and recorded.

Applies to: Cape Town, Durban, Gqeberha and Polokwane

Procedure owner: IT Manager

Final purchasing approval: Company management, after technical and budget reviews

Status: Proposed procedure for the African Foods assignment

1. Purpose and scope

This procedure covers requests for computers, printers, network equipment, accessories and software licences. It applies to new purchases, replacements and upgrades across all four locations.

The purpose is to control spending, avoid unnecessary purchases and make sure that equipment is suitable for the company's applications and network.

Employees must obtain the required approval before placing an order or committing company funds. An urgent request still needs authorised approval.

2. Prepare your equipment request

Give your manager and IT enough information to assess the request. Include the following details:

  • Requester: Your name, department and branch.
  • Equipment needed: The type of item and quantity required.
  • Intended user: The employee or team that will use the equipment.
  • Business reason: Explain the work the equipment must support and the problem with the current arrangement.
  • Required date: State when the equipment is needed and explain any time-sensitive business requirement.
  • Existing asset: For a replacement or upgrade, include the current asset number, condition and relevant support ticket reference, where available.
  • Application requirements: Identify the approved applications or services that the equipment must support.

3. Equipment acquisition procedure

  1. Identify and document the need

    The requesting employee explains what is needed and how it will support their work. A request should describe the business problem rather than only name a preferred product.

  2. Obtain the manager's confirmation

    The department or branch manager checks that the request is necessary and confirms the intended user and required date. Incomplete requests are returned for clarification.

  3. Check available alternatives

    IT checks whether repairing, upgrading or reassigning existing equipment could meet the requirement before recommending a new purchase.

  4. Assess technical suitability

    IT checks compatibility with approved software, security requirements and network connections. The assessment must also consider capacity, support needs and available software licences.

  5. Obtain and compare quotations

    The employee responsible for purchasing obtains comparable quotations from suitable suppliers. The comparison should consider price, warranty, delivery, support and ongoing operating costs.

  6. Review the technical recommendation

    The IT Manager reviews the assessment and recommends an appropriate option. The cheapest item should only be selected if it meets the approved requirements.

  7. Confirm budget availability

    Finance checks that funds are available and identifies the appropriate budget allocation. If funding is unavailable, the request must be revised, deferred or referred for a decision.

  8. Obtain final approval

    Company management approves or declines the expenditure after reviewing the business need, technical recommendation and budget position. The decision must be recorded.

  9. Place the order

    The authorised purchasing employee issues the purchase order using the approved specification, quantity and supplier. The order must be linked to the approved request.

  10. Inspect the delivery

    The receiving employee and IT compare delivered items with the order. They check quantities, model details, serial numbers and visible damage before accepting the equipment.

  11. Configure and test the equipment

    IT installs approved software and applies the required settings. Testing must confirm that the equipment supports its intended tasks, such as printing or accessing authorised company applications.

  12. Record, hand over and close the request

    IT records the asset, location, assigned user and warranty details. The user confirms receipt and receives basic instructions. Purchasing and acceptance records are retained together.

4. Who is responsible?

Proposed responsibilities for equipment acquisition
Role Responsibility
Requesting employee Explains the requirement, supplies supporting details and confirms receipt of the equipment.
Department or branch manager Confirms the business need, intended user and priority of the request.
IT technical staff Assess existing equipment and compatibility, prepare the device, complete testing and update asset records.
IT Manager Reviews the technical recommendation and checks that it supports the company's IT requirements.
Finance Confirms available funding and the correct budget allocation.
Company management Gives final expenditure approval or records the reason for declining or deferring the request.
Authorised purchasing employee Obtains quotations, issues the approved purchase order and coordinates supplier and delivery records.

5. Workplace examples

Select an example to see how the procedure applies.

A Durban salesperson's computer repeatedly stops working.

The employee should first report the fault to the help desk. IT investigates and records whether repair or an upgrade is practical. If replacement is recommended, the equipment request should refer to the support record and explain the effect on processing sales orders.

The Gqeberha branch needs another workstation.

The branch manager should confirm the intended user and business need. IT checks whether a suitable device is already available and assesses software, licence and connection requirements before recommending a purchase.

An employee finds a discounted laptop online.

The employee must not buy it using company funds without approval. IT must first assess whether the specification, warranty and support arrangements meet the company's requirements.

Delivered equipment does not match the purchase order.

The receiving employee must record the difference and notify the authorised purchaser and IT. The item must not be treated as an accepted replacement without the required review.

6. Records and equipment handover

The completed acquisition record should allow the company to trace why an item was purchased, who approved it and where it is being used.

  • The original request and business justification.
  • The technical assessment and supplier quotations.
  • Budget confirmation and recorded approvals.
  • The purchase order, delivery details and invoice.
  • The asset number, model, serial number and location.
  • The assigned user, warranty and relevant licence details.
  • Testing results and confirmation of handover.

Replaced equipment must be assessed for secure reuse or disposal. Authorised IT staff must handle company data before a device is reassigned or leaves the company.

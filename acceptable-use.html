WORK RESPONSIBLY

Good technology starts with responsible use.

Our computers, email and network help employees process orders, manage stock and communicate between branches. This policy explains how these resources should be used.

Applies to: Cape Town, Durban, Gqeberha and Polokwane

Policy owner: IT Manager

Status: Proposed policy for the African Foods assignment

1. Purpose and scope

This policy applies to employees and authorised contractors using African Foods computers, applications, email, internet access, printers and shared storage.

Its purpose is to support business activities, protect company information and explain the responsibilities of each person using these resources.

2. Responsible use of company resources

  • Use technology for authorised work. Company resources should primarily support activities such as sales, shipping, stock control, payroll and communication between branches.
  • Use approved applications. Employees must use software approved for their duties and follow its licence conditions. Requests for additional software must be referred to IT.
  • Communicate professionally. Check the recipient, message and attachments before sending an email, especially when sharing customer, order or employee information.
  • Respect access limits. Only use information needed for your work. Access to a shared folder does not give permission to use its contents for unrelated purposes.
  • Protect confidential information. Keep business documents in approved storage locations. Obtain approval before sharing company records with an external person or service.
  • Use shared equipment responsibly. Collect confidential printouts promptly. Avoid unnecessary printing and duplicate files that take up shared storage.
  • Follow personal-device rules. Personal laptops, phones and storage devices may access company resources only when authorised and under the conditions set by IT.
  • Keep business work available. Non-work internet activity must not interrupt duties or reduce the connection capacity needed by other employees.

3. Activities that are not allowed

  • Sharing passwords, using another employee's account or attempting to bypass access restrictions.
  • Installing unapproved software, browser extensions or remote-access tools.
  • Using company resources for illegal activities, harassment or downloading pirated material.
  • Uploading company records to personal email, personal cloud storage or public websites without approval.
  • Deliberately accessing, changing or deleting information without authorisation.
  • Disabling security controls or connecting unapproved equipment to the company network.
  • Using company equipment to attack or disrupt another device, network or organisation.

4. What does this mean at work?

Select an example to read the appropriate action.

A Durban salesperson wants to email a customer list to their personal account.

The salesperson should not transfer the list without approval. They should ask their manager and IT for an approved way to access the information needed for work.

A shipping clerk receives an unexpected attachment about an order.

The clerk should check whether the message is expected and report anything suspicious through the approved support channel. They should not open the attachment simply because it mentions a delivery.

A Polokwane employee wants to install a free application.

The employee should explain the business need to IT before installing it. IT must assess compatibility, security and licence conditions. An application being free does not automatically make it suitable.

An employee notices confidential documents at a printer.

The employee should avoid reading or sharing the documents and notify the responsible person. Employees printing confidential material should collect it promptly.

5. Reporting mistakes and suspected misuse

Report misdirected emails, accidental disclosure, suspicious downloads and suspected misuse promptly. Explain what happened, when it happened and which device or service was involved.

Employees must not hide incidents or attempt an unauthorised repair. The help desk records the report and escalates it to the IT Manager where appropriate.

6. Monitoring and accountability

Authorised monitoring should be limited to legitimate operational and security purposes and follow the company's approved privacy and employment procedures.

Employees are responsible for their use of company resources. The IT Manager refers suspected misuse to the appropriate manager for review. Any further action must follow approved company procedures.

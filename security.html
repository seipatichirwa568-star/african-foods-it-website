PROTECT YOUR ACCOUNT. PROTECT YOUR WORK.

Small habits help protect the whole company.

Employees across African Foods use technology to handle orders, stock, customer information and administrative records. This policy explains how accounts, devices and information should be protected.

Applies to: Cape Town, Durban, Gqeberha and Polokwane

Policy owner: IT Manager

Status: Proposed policy for the African Foods assignment

1. Purpose and scope

This proposed policy applies to employees, authorised contractors and company devices at all four African Foods locations.

Its purpose is to reduce unauthorised access, protect business information and explain what employees must do when they notice a possible security problem.

The IT Manager oversees the policy. IT staff implement approved controls, while employees follow the rules and report concerns promptly.

2. Protect accounts and control access

  • Use your assigned account. Employees must use their own authorised login. Do not work through another person's account or allow colleagues to use yours.
  • Keep passwords private. Do not share passwords with colleagues or include them in emails and support reports. IT must use an approved account-recovery process when assistance is needed.
  • Use unique passwords. Follow the company's approved password requirements and avoid reusing a company password for personal accounts.
  • Use additional sign-in protection. Where multifactor authentication is enabled, follow the approved sign-in process. Do not approve an unexpected authentication request.
  • Access information according to your duties. Access must be approved for the employee's work. For example, a shipping clerk should not automatically receive access to confidential HR or payroll records.
  • Request access changes properly. Managers must notify IT when employees join, change roles or leave. IT updates access following an authorised instruction and records the action.

3. Protect devices and use approved software

  • Lock your screen. Lock the workstation whenever you leave your desk. On Windows, use Windows + L.
  • Keep equipment physically secure. Protect company laptops and mobile devices when travelling or working away from the office. Report lost or stolen equipment immediately.
  • Install only approved software. Ask IT before adding applications, browser extensions or remote-access tools. Do not disable company security controls.
  • Use authorised connections. Do not connect unknown storage devices or unapproved equipment to company systems. Follow IT instructions for wireless and remote access.
  • Cooperate with updates. IT must manage approved updates and maintenance. Employees should follow the communicated instructions and avoid interrupting installations without guidance.
  • Restrict access to infrastructure. Only authorised personnel may access servers and network equipment. Approved visitors requiring access must be supervised.

4. Protect company information

  • Use approved storage. Save business documents in the locations provided for company work so that approved access and backup arrangements can apply.
  • Check before sharing. Confirm recipients and permissions before sending customer, financial or employee records. Do not transfer these records to personal accounts or unapproved services.
  • Handle printed records carefully. Collect confidential printouts promptly and keep them secure when they are not being used.
  • Be cautious with messages. Do not open suspicious links or attachments or provide information to an unverified sender. Report concerns through the approved support channel.
  • Follow backup procedures. IT is responsible for approved backup operations. Employees must keep business information in approved locations and report missing or damaged records.
  • Refer device disposal to IT. Company devices must be assessed by authorised staff before reassignment or disposal so that company information is handled appropriately.

5. Report security concerns promptly

Do not wait for a small concern to become a bigger incident.

Report suspected account compromise, unexpected sign-in requests, suspicious messages, missing devices and accidental information disclosure through an available approved channel.

  1. Describe what happened

    Provide your branch, the affected device or account, the time and the symptoms. Do not include passwords or unnecessary confidential information.

  2. Follow authorised instructions

    Allow IT to assess the situation and guide the response. Avoid installing unapproved repair tools or making unrelated changes.

  3. Keep relevant details available

    Retain information that may help the investigation and follow IT instructions about handling suspicious messages or files. Do not forward harmful attachments to colleagues.

  4. Confirm the outcome

    Cooperate with authorised checks and report any recurring symptoms. Resume affected activities according to the guidance provided by IT.

6. Security responsibilities

Proposed responsibilities for applying the security policy
Role Responsibility
Employees Follow approved rules, protect accounts and devices, and report suspected security problems promptly.
Department and branch managers Confirm appropriate access requirements and notify IT about staff movements and business changes.
IT Support Technician Records reports, assists through approved support processes and escalates security concerns promptly.
Network Administrator Maintains approved network controls, investigates relevant technical faults and assists with authorised containment and recovery.
IT Manager Oversees the policy, coordinates significant incidents and refers suspected breaches through approved management and HR procedures.

The policy should be reviewed after significant incidents or changes to company systems and working arrangements.

7. Workplace examples

Select an example to read the appropriate action.

Someone claiming to be IT asks for your password.

Do not provide it. Verify the request through the company's approved support channel. Legitimate account assistance should use an authorised recovery process rather than password sharing.

You receive a sign-in approval request you did not start.

Do not approve the request. Report the unexpected activity promptly and follow IT instructions for checking and protecting your account.

You find a USB drive near the office printer.

Do not connect it to your computer to identify its owner. Give it to authorised IT staff and explain where it was found.

A company laptop is lost while travelling.

Report the loss immediately through an available approved channel. Provide the device details, when it was last seen and any other relevant facts. IT will coordinate the appropriate account and device response.

You accidentally email a customer record to the wrong person.

Report the mistake promptly and provide the relevant message details through an approved channel. Do not assume that deleting the message from your own mailbox removes the recipient's copy.

PREPARE. RESPOND. RECOVER.

Be ready when normal work is interrupted.

A serious IT incident can interrupt sales, shipping, stock control and communication between branches. This plan explains who should respond and how African Foods should restore its services.

Applies to: Cape Town, Durban, Gqeberha and Polokwane

Recovery coordinator: IT Manager

Status: Proposed plan for the African Foods assignment

1. First response and recovery priorities

People come first

Follow the company's emergency and evacuation instructions. Employees must not enter an unsafe building or handle damaged electrical equipment to recover company data.

  1. Report the incident. Tell the help desk or IT Manager which branch, devices and services are affected. Use an available approved alternative channel if the network is down.
  2. Assess the impact. The IT team checks whether the problem affects one employee, one branch or central services used by several locations.
  3. Coordinate the response. The IT Manager decides whether to activate the recovery plan, assigns responsibilities and updates company management and branch contacts.
  4. Contain further damage. Authorised staff take appropriate action to prevent the incident from spreading or causing additional loss.
  5. Restore services in a planned order. Restore the infrastructure needed by critical applications before testing the applications and employee access that depend on it.
  6. Verify before returning to normal work. Technical staff and authorised users check that services and information are usable before the IT Manager confirms their release.

The Cape Town infrastructure requires particular attention because other branches depend on its central services. The exact recovery order must reflect the incident and the affected business tasks.

3. Incident recovery plans

Select a situation to read its proposed response. These instructions guide authorised staff; this website does not perform recovery actions.

Server or database failure

Business impact: A Cape Town server failure could prevent employees at several branches from accessing central information and applications.

Responsible roles: IT Manager, the responsible administrator and an approved specialist where required.

  1. Record the affected services, symptoms and users, and notify branch contacts.
  2. Investigate whether the fault involves power, hardware, storage, connectivity or the hosted service.
  3. Repair the fault or prepare an approved replacement environment as appropriate.
  4. Where restoration is needed, use a suitable verified backup and record its recovery point.
  5. Ask authorised users to check that the application opens, expected records are available and normal tasks can be completed.
  6. Identify and reconcile missing transactions using available records. Report any unrecoverable information.
Hardware or network equipment failure

Business impact: A failed workstation may affect one employee, while a failed switch or router may disconnect an entire branch.

Responsible roles: IT Support Technician for workstations and Network Administrator for network equipment.

  1. Identify the affected equipment and confirm the extent of the disruption.
  2. Carry out approved checks and remove unsafe or failed equipment from use.
  3. Repair the item or use a compatible spare where available. Obtain purchasing approval if replacement equipment is required.
  4. Restore approved settings and required software or network configurations.
  5. Test the intended functions with affected users and update the asset and incident records.
Internet or inter-branch connection outage

Business impact: A branch may lose access to remote services even when its local computers are still working.

Responsible roles: Network Administrator and the relevant service provider.

  1. Determine whether the failure affects local networking, internet access or the connection to central company services.
  2. Check device status, physical connections and the relevant communication paths.
  3. Report an external connection fault to the provider and keep the incident reference and progress updates.
  4. Use an alternative connection only if it is available, approved and suitable for secure access to the required services.
  5. After restoration, test access from the affected branch and confirm that staff can resume their business tasks.
Power interruption

Business impact: Computers, network equipment and central services may become unavailable.

Responsible roles: IT Manager and authorised technical staff, working with the responsible site personnel.

  1. Protect employee safety and report the interruption through an available channel.
  2. Where suitable UPS equipment is provided, use it within its capacity to support an orderly shutdown or short-term operation.
  3. Shut down affected systems safely when continued operation is not possible.
  4. Avoid repeatedly restarting devices while the power supply remains unstable.
  5. Once power is stable, restore infrastructure in a controlled order.
  6. Check applications, network access, backup jobs and any work interrupted by the outage.
Cyberattack or suspected malware

Business impact: An affected device or account could put company information and other systems at risk.

Responsible roles: IT Manager, authorised technical staff and approved security specialists where required.

  1. Report unusual activity immediately and follow authorised IT instructions.
  2. Isolate affected devices or accounts as appropriate to limit further spread.
  3. Retain relevant logs and incident details. Avoid unnecessary changes that could remove useful evidence.
  4. Investigate and address the cause before reconnecting affected systems.
  5. Recover using trusted configurations and backups assessed as suitable for restoration.
  6. Verify services, monitor for recurring activity and record improvements needed to reduce the chance of another incident.
Accidental deletion or corrupted information

Business impact: Employees may lose access to documents or records required for their work.

Responsible roles: An authorised technician and the owner of the affected information.

  1. Report the file or system, storage location and approximate time the loss occurred.
  2. Avoid further changes to the affected information until IT provides guidance.
  3. Identify an appropriate backup or available previous version.
  4. Where possible, restore to a separate location first so that current information is not overwritten without checking.
  5. Ask the information owner to confirm that the recovered content is correct.
  6. Record the recovery result, including any information that remains missing.
Fire, flood or loss of access to a building

Business impact: Staff may lose safe access to equipment or an entire company location.

Responsible roles: Company management and the IT Manager, following emergency authority instructions.

  1. Follow emergency and evacuation instructions. Do not enter an unsafe building to recover equipment or documents.
  2. Assess damage only when the appropriate authorities permit access.
  3. Identify the systems that need to be restored and the equipment required.
  4. Use recoverable backups and documentation held outside the affected location.
  5. Establish an approved temporary working arrangement and test replacement equipment and connectivity before use.
  6. Communicate service limitations and reconcile work recorded during the disruption.

4. Temporary working arrangements

The Operations Officer should coordinate an approved way to record essential work while systems are unavailable. For example, staff may use controlled order records rather than keeping separate, untracked notes.

  • Give each temporary record a unique reference, date and responsible employee.
  • Record the information needed for later entry without collecting unnecessary confidential details.
  • Keep temporary records secure and accessible only to authorised employees.
  • Do not assume that stock availability or an order's status is confirmed when the relevant system cannot be checked.
  • After restoration, reconcile and enter outstanding work carefully to avoid duplicate orders or incorrect stock records.

5. Backups and recovery preparation

Our group proposes protected backups of critical company information and network device configurations, including a recoverable copy outside the main site. Access must be limited to authorised staff.

  • Identify what needs protection. Include critical records, required system information and approved device configurations.
  • Agree on recovery targets. Management and IT must decide how much downtime and data loss each critical service can tolerate. These decisions guide backup frequency and recovery resources.
  • Check backup results. Investigate failed or incomplete backup jobs and keep a record of corrective action.
  • Test restoration. Perform the proposed quarterly sample restoration test in a safe environment and record whether the recovered information or service is usable.
  • Keep recovery information accessible. Maintain current network diagrams, configuration records and approved provider contact details outside the affected systems.
  • Review after changes or incidents. Update the plan when equipment, responsibilities or business requirements change.

A successful backup message alone does not prove that a working service can be restored. Recovery targets must not be presented as guaranteed times until the arrangements have been tested.

6. Checks before reopening services

The IT Manager should confirm that the relevant technical and business checks have been completed before normal operations resume.

  • The immediate cause has been addressed or an approved temporary solution is in place.
  • Required infrastructure and application connections are available.
  • Authorised employees can sign in and carry out the affected business tasks.
  • Restored information has been checked by its owner and any missing records identified.
  • Outstanding transactions have been reconciled or assigned for follow-up.
  • Branch contacts have received an update explaining restored services and remaining limitations.
  • The incident record includes actions taken, recovery results and improvements required.

CHECK. MAINTAIN. DOCUMENT.

Keep everyday work running smoothly.

Regular maintenance helps the IT team identify problems early, protect important information and manage changes without unnecessary disruption.

Applies to: Cape Town, Durban, Gqeberha and Polokwane

Procedure owner: IT Manager

Main technical roles: Network Administrator and IT Support Technician

Status: Proposed procedure for the African Foods assignment

1. Purpose and scope

This procedure covers routine checks and authorised maintenance of network devices, central services, wireless access, backups and related access records.

African Foods depends on communication between its branches and the Cape Town head office. Maintenance should therefore consider both the local effect of a change and its effect on other locations.

Our group proposes a maintenance schedule that assigns tasks to specific roles. The schedule should be adjusted after the company reviews its workload and operational needs.

2. Proposed maintenance schedule

Maintenance frequency, activities and responsible roles

This schedule is a proposed working arrangement. Urgent faults and significant security concerns must be assessed when reported rather than left until the next scheduled review.

3. What the maintenance checks involve

  • Routers and switches: Review device and interface status, investigate unexpected disconnections and compare relevant settings with the approved configuration records.
  • Central services: Check that required services are available and review reported faults, storage capacity and relevant alerts.
  • Wireless access: Review access point status and investigate connection or coverage problems reported by employees.
  • Configuration backups: Confirm that approved device configurations have recoverable copies and that authorised staff can locate them when needed.
  • Backup results: Investigate failed or incomplete backup jobs. Record corrective action and include restoration testing in the proposed quarterly checks.
  • Updates: Assess applicable updates, compatibility and potential business disruption before scheduling authorised installation.
  • Physical connections: Inspect accessible cables and connections during relevant checks or site visits. Escalate damaged or unsafe equipment.
  • User access: Review access with the responsible managers. Update or remove access following authorised instructions when employees change roles or leave.
  • Documentation: Keep asset records, network diagrams and configuration information consistent with approved changes.

4. Planning and completing maintenance changes

Maintenance that may interrupt services must be approved by the IT Manager. Branch contacts must receive advance notice explaining which services will be affected and when work is expected to take place.

  1. Define the work

    The implementing technician identifies the affected equipment, the reason for the change and the expected result.

  2. Assess the business impact

    Identify which branches, users and services may be interrupted. Consider important activities such as order processing before selecting a maintenance window.

  3. Prepare the recovery method

    Back up the affected configuration and document how to return to the previous approved state if the change fails. Check that the required recovery resources are available.

  4. Obtain approval and notify users

    Submit the proposed work to the IT Manager where approval is required. Inform branch contacts of the expected interruption, planned timing and reporting arrangements.

  5. Carry out the approved work

    Follow the agreed steps and record the actions taken. Avoid adding unrelated changes during the maintenance window.

  6. Test the affected services

    Check the relevant connections and applications. Ask affected users to confirm that their normal tasks can be completed.

  7. Record the result and communicate closure

    Update the maintenance record and relevant documentation. Inform branch contacts when services are available and explain any remaining limitations or follow-up work.

If the change causes a serious problem

The technician must stop further changes, inform the IT Manager and use the approved rollback method where appropriate. The maintenance record must state what was changed, the checks completed and any follow-up work required.

5. Information to record

A maintenance record helps the next technician understand what happened and provides evidence for reviewing repeated problems.

  • The date, branch and affected device or service.
  • The reason for the work and the related incident or change reference.
  • The technician responsible and any required approval.
  • The configuration backup reference and planned rollback method.
  • The work performed, including relevant settings or software versions changed.
  • The tests completed and their actual results.
  • User confirmation, outstanding limitations and assigned follow-up actions.

Maintenance records must not contain passwords. Configuration backups and other sensitive technical records must be kept in approved restricted locations.

6. Workplace examples

Select an example to see how the procedure applies.

A change to the Cape Town router could affect every branch.

The Network Administrator should identify the affected connections, back up the configuration and prepare a rollback method. The IT Manager must approve disruptive work, and branch contacts should receive advance notice.

After the change, the administrator should test the affected paths and confirm with branch users that required services remain accessible.

The daily review shows that a backup failed.

The Network Administrator should investigate the failure, record its cause where known and take approved corrective action. The issue should not be treated as resolved until the relevant result has been checked.

If the failure leaves important information without adequate recovery coverage, the IT Manager must be informed.

A Polokwane employee leaves the company.

The relevant manager should send an authorised instruction to IT. The IT Support Technician updates access and equipment records according to the approved timing and retains a record of the actions completed.

This is handled when the staff change occurs, rather than waiting for the quarterly access review.

An update causes an application to stop working.

The technician should stop further changes, record the symptoms and inform the IT Manager. The approved rollback method should be used where appropriate, followed by checks with affected users.

The failed change and any further investigation must be documented before another attempt.

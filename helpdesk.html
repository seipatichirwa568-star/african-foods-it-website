REPORT. RESOLVE. RESUME.

Get the right help to keep work moving.

A clear problem report helps IT investigate more quickly. This guide explains how African Foods employees should report faults, how requests are prioritised and when problems are escalated.

Applies to: Cape Town, Durban, Gqeberha and Polokwane

First point of contact: IT Support Technician / Help Desk

Procedure owner: IT Manager

Status: Proposed procedure for the African Foods assignment

1. Reporting a technical problem

Employees should report computer, printer, application and network problems through the company's approved help desk channel. Avoid making major changes to devices or network settings without IT instructions.

If a network outage prevents normal reporting, the nominated branch contact should use the agreed alternative telephone channel and explain the effect on the branch.

Suspected security incident?

Report suspicious activity, lost devices or suspected account compromise immediately. Do not wait for routine troubleshooting or share your password with anyone.

This academic website provides guidance. It does not submit support tickets or contact an IT team. Real support contact details must be supplied by the company before operational use.

2. Information to include in your report

  • Your details: Name, department, branch and an approved way for IT to contact you.
  • Affected equipment: The device name or asset number, where available.
  • Affected service: Identify the application, printer, connection or account involved.
  • Problem description: Explain what you were trying to do and what happened instead.
  • Error details: Include the exact error message or a screenshot that does not reveal confidential information.
  • Time and frequency: State when the problem started and whether it happens continuously or occasionally.
  • Business impact: Explain which work has stopped, how many people are affected and whether a workaround exists.
  • Checks already completed: Describe any approved steps you have tried. Do not include passwords or unnecessary customer or employee records.

3. Help desk procedure

  1. Receive the report

    The IT Support Technician collects the employee's details and checks that the problem description is clear.

  2. Create a support record

    The help desk assigns a unique ticket reference and records the branch, device, symptoms and reporting time. Reports linked to the same outage should be connected.

  3. Assess impact and urgency

    The technician determines how many employees are affected, which business activity is interrupted and whether a temporary workaround is available.

  4. Assign and investigate

    A suitable technician carries out approved troubleshooting and records the checks performed. Any changes must remain within the technician's authority.

  5. Escalate when necessary

    Network faults that cannot be resolved through first-line checks are referred to the Network Administrator. Security concerns and major disruptions are reported to the IT Manager promptly.

  6. Keep employees informed

    The help desk communicates progress, outstanding issues and approved workarounds. Branch-wide updates should be coordinated through the nominated branch contact.

  7. Test and confirm the solution

    The technician tests the affected service and asks the employee to confirm that the original task can now be completed.

  8. Close and review the ticket

    Record the cause where known, actions taken, test results and user confirmation. Repeated incidents should be reviewed to identify whether a permanent correction is needed.

4. How incidents are prioritised

Priority depends on business impact and urgency. The following categories are proposed for African Foods; they are not guaranteed response or resolution times.

Proposed help desk priority categories
Priority Example Required response
Critical Central services are unavailable to several branches, or a suspected serious security incident occurs. Escalate immediately to the IT Manager and relevant technical staff. Assess whether coordinated recovery is required.
High An entire branch loses access, or time-sensitive business work stops without a suitable workaround. Arrange urgent investigation and communicate updates to the affected employees.
Normal One employee has a device or application fault and a reasonable workaround is available. Assign the incident through the normal support queue and confirm the workaround is suitable.
Low An employee requests general guidance or a planned service that does not interrupt current work. Schedule assistance according to workload and business requirements.

The help desk must review the priority if the impact changes. A problem affecting one employee may still require urgent attention if it stops a critical task.

5. Escalation responsibilities

  • Junior IT Support Technician: Assists with basic checks under supervision and refers unfamiliar problems to the IT Support Technician.
  • IT Support Technician: Owns the support record, performs first-line troubleshooting and keeps the employee informed.
  • Network Administrator: Investigates escalated routing, switching, wireless and central connectivity problems.
  • IT Manager: Coordinates major incidents, security concerns, resource decisions and management communication.
  • Approved service provider: Assists with external connection faults or specialist problems referred by authorised IT staff.

An escalation must include the ticket reference, symptoms, business impact and checks already performed. The help desk remains responsible for coordinating user updates unless another owner is explicitly assigned.

6. Workplace examples

Select an example to see how the procedure applies.

All Durban employees cannot access the central system.

The branch contact reports the shared problem and identifies the affected work. The help desk links related reports and refers the connection fault to the Network Administrator.

This would normally be treated as high priority. If other branches or central services are also affected, the priority should be reassessed.

One employee cannot print, but another approved printer is available.

The help desk records the device, printer and error details. The technician checks the fault and confirms whether the alternative printer is suitable for the employee's work.

The incident may be handled as normal priority while the approved workaround is available.

An employee notices unexpected account activity.

The employee reports it immediately without sharing their password. The help desk refers the concern to the IT Manager and authorised technical staff for assessment and containment.

The same application fault keeps returning.

The technician links the earlier ticket references and records the repeated symptoms. The IT team should investigate the underlying cause rather than repeatedly closing the same fault without a lasting solution.
